Job Market Paper

Does the Rating Matter? Rating Wedge, Persistent Debt, and Financial Fragility

Abstract. Credit ratings can shape corporate risk through financing conditions. Using Norwegian firm data, I compare the public rating with a fundamentals-based rating. Within a public rating grade, the rating wedge predicts higher next-year bankruptcy risk and greater liabilities; conditional on the fundamentals-based rating, a more favorable public rating is associated with lower financing costs. I develop a model to explain these empirical patterns. The model shows how favorable ratings encourage borrowing and how the resulting debt can outlive the rating advantage, creating delayed balance-sheet fragility. Quantitatively, this channel is concentrated among highly leveraged firms near default, while its aggregate effect is modest.

How Environmental Policy Affects Agriculture: Evidence from China’s Sulfur Dioxide Control Zone Policy

with Zichen Deng

Abstract. We develop a new approach to evaluating environmental policies in settings where pollution spills across administrative boundaries. Our approach integrates atmospheric particle-trajectory data with natural-experiment designs to construct a measure of policy exposure at affected locations, regardless of whether they lie inside or outside regulated jurisdictions. This addresses a key limitation of conventional jurisdiction-based designs, which may misclassify treated locations when pollution travels across borders. Applying the method to China’s Two Control Zones policy, we show that conventional estimates become increasingly attenuated when cross-boundary spillovers are important, while our approach better captures the policy effect. When spillovers are limited, the two approaches perform similarly.

What Text Helps Forecast Inflation? Evidence from Retrieval-Augmented Language Models

with Yuan Lin

Abstract. Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly studied in macroeconomic forecasting. They can use qualitative information from central bank communications and financial news that is difficult to incorporate into standard econometric models. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) supplies the model with documents available at the time of forecasting, including information published after its training. We use RAG to forecast U.S. Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation one, three, six, and twelve months ahead. The system retrieves and filters passages from central bank communications and financial news, then supplies them to the LLM alongside historical CPI data in a structured prompt. Over the January 2024–February 2026 evaluation period, the best-performing RAG configurations improve on conventional econometric benchmarks mainly at six and twelve months. The relative performance of the text sources varies across horizons: central bank communications are more useful at short horizons, while financial news contributes more at longer horizons.
